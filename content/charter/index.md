## 1. Background

The **Brain Behavior Quantification and Synchronization (BBQS)** program is a basic research effort to develop new tools and approaches in support of a more comprehensive mechanistic understanding of the neural basis of behavior. The **Data Coordination and Artificial Intelligence Center (DCAIC)**(pronounced as *dee-cake*) will act as a data management and sharing hub for BBQS projects. DCAIC will oversee activities in five interrelated categories: 1. Data Management, 2. Data Standards, 3. Machine learning (ML) and artificial intelligence (AI) Resources, 4. Data Ecosystem, 5. Dissemination, Training, and Coordination.

**Working groups** are essential to the success of a consortium project, as they bring together a range of experts from different organizations to focus on specific tasks. Each group works on a particular area of the project, collaborating to find solutions and share knowledge. By dividing the project into smaller, more manageable parts, working groups help ensure that work moves forward smoothly and deadlines are met. Together, their efforts contribute to the overall goals of the project, ensuring it achieves its desired outcomes. Working groups may also create **task forces** to further investigate and create responses for specific scoped tasks.

## 2. Goals

The Working Group on Data Standards aims to establish and promote the adoption of data standards for novel sensors and multimodal data integration within the BBQS community. This effort is essential to facilitate FAIR (Findable, Accessible, Interoperable, Reusable) sharing and reuse of brain behavior data, ensuring consistency, interoperability, and integration of data and metadata to support scientific research and collaboration.

1. **Year 1: Inventory Behavior Data Standards** to enable the BBQS community to effectively leverage and use existing standards, ontologies, methods and tools:
   1. **Goal:** Identify and review existing data standards relevant to BBQS studies. Summarize and evaluate BBQS relevant data standards to understand their applicability and gaps.
   2. **Deliverable:** Publish an organized and categorized catalog of standards for BBQS studies accessible via the BBQS web portal.
   3. **Stretch goal:** Engage with the relevant data standards to promote enhancements to address relevant gaps.
2. **Develop Guidelines and Best Practices** to help BBQS users understand which standards are relevant to their research and how to best use them, by:
   1. Identifying where ‘fault lines’ lie between standards to clarify which standards are most appropriate for what types of data and developing recommendations to clarify which standard(s) are best suited for BBQS.
   2. Formulating use-case driven minimum information (MI) requirement guidelines for BBQS data.
3. **Coordinate and Promote Enhancement of Standards** to promote improvement of existing data standards to better meet BBQS needs by:
   1. Facilitating the organization of community working groups to develop strategies and enhancements to standards.
   2. Collaborating with standards bodies to update standards for BBQS and ensure compliance with neuroethics principles.
   3. Creating crosswalks to identify common metadata fields across standards and link them to existing ontologies.
4. **Facilitate Adoption and Use of BBQS Standards** by working with BBQS and data standards bodies and teams to:
   1. Create online tutorials and best practices to train BBQS teams in applying guidelines and best practices.
   2. Develop curated controlled term sets for BBQS, leveraging existing ontologies and resources.
   3. Develop mechanisms for annotating existing data standards to ease the adoption of BBQS data guidelines.

### 2.2 Out of Scope

1. **Development of New Data Standards:** The working group will not develop new data standards but will focus on leveraging and enhancing existing standards. To ensure the BBQS community can effectively share data that requires new standards the team may:
   1. Engage with respective data standard governing bodies and the broader community to initiate the creation of community-led working groups to develop targeted extensions to existing data standards.
   2. Develop interim guidelines for sharing affected data types, e.g., by defining how more generic data types may be used to describe the data for sharing, while more specific standards are being developed by the community.
2. **Implementation of Data Management Tools**: The working group will not develop or implement data management tools or software but will collaborate with relevant parties to ensure standards are supported.If data management tool needs are identified, the team will:
   1. Coordinate as appropriate with the “Data Management” thrust and working groups of the DCAIC and the EMBER projects..
3. **Direct Data Collection and Analysis:** The working group will not engage in the direct collection or scientific analysis of research data but will provide guidelines and standards for others to do so. If data analysis needs are identified as part of this working group, the team will:
   1. Coordinate as appropriate with the “Data Analysis” thrust and working groups of the DCAIC.

## 3. Organization

### 3.1: Working Group Leadership

The working group will be led by a Chair and Co-Chair, responsible for organizing meetings, setting agendas, and ensuring progress towards goals.

### 3.2 Membership and Expectations

The working group will consist of subject matter experts, data scientists, and members of the BBQS community. Membership is open to individuals and organizations interested in contributing to the development and adoption of data standards.

* **Google Group:** [https://groups.google.com/u/0/a/brain-bbqs.org/g/wg-standards/members](https://groups.google.com/u/0/a/brain-bbqs.org/g/wg-standards/members)
* **Current Members: [Working Group Assignments](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/u/5/d/19vy3bOUzgBzCgfmWh0s2pjJD2Tq3XMC-HvEkVBEPU38/edit)**

#### 3.2.1 Expectations

Members are expected to:

* Participate in monthly 1-hour working meetings and occasional discussions about working group strategies via email and working group documents.
* Additional responsibilities will be voluntary and decided on jointly by the working group, e.g.:
  * Participation in organization and/or provide feedback on scheduling of working group events
  * Participation in reporting of working group results to the community and BBQS leadership
  * Participation in and leadership of subcommittees as decided on by the working group

### 3.3 Task Forces

The working group may form task forces to focus on specific tasks or areas of expertise, such as guidelines development, community engagement, and standards enhancement. The task forces will report to the main working group on regular intervals to facilitate overall coordination of efforts.

### 3.4 Meetings

* The working group will **meet monthly,** with additional meetings scheduled as needed. Subcommittees may meet more frequently as required to accomplish their tasks.
* Meetings will be **held virtually** to accommodate members from different geographical locations.
* **Agendas and minutes** will be distributed in advance to ensure productive discussions.
* At the beginning of each meeting, the meeting lead may select a **scribe** for the meeting

### 3.5 Decision-Making Process

Decisions will be made collaboratively, with consensus as the primary goal. The Chair will facilitate discussions to ensure that all perspectives are considered and that decisions reflect the collective input of the group. In instances where consensus cannot be reached after reasonable efforts, the Chair will guide further discussion to identify potential compromises or alternative solutions. If consensus remains unattainable, the Chair may seek input or final guidance from NIH and DCAIC leadership to resolve the matter.
